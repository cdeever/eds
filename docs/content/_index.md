# EdS

<p class="subtitle"><em>Vinyl the way it was cut, in a room that listens along.</em></p>

EdS is where the old analog world meets the convenience of the new one.

Put a record on and nothing about the sound changes. The signal runs from
the stylus to the speakers without ever becoming a number. Everything EdS
adds happens beside that path, never in it.

What it adds is a room that knows what is playing.

## The sound is left alone

For the purist, this is the whole point. The path from stylus to speakers is
analog from end to end, and EdS is not in it. It listens from a tap off to
the side, the way a tape deck would. Switch EdS off and the stereo plays
exactly as it did before.

## The light follows the sleeve

Prop the jacket on its stand and the stand lights up from it. EdS reads the
cover art, draws out its colors, and sets them moving behind the sleeve: a
slow wash for one record, a chase of accents for the next. The room takes on
the mood of the album without anyone choosing a thing.

When the music stops, the light settles to a low, warm glow.

## A screen that belongs to the stereo

Beside the turntable is a small touch screen with one job. It shows the
cover of whatever is playing. Touch it and it becomes a remote: play, pause,
skip, louder, quieter. It is always where you are standing, and it is never
busy being anything else.

With a record on, it is simply the sleeve. Nothing can skip a record, so it
does not pretend to.

## It knows without being told

- **Play a record** and EdS hears a few seconds of it, names the track, then
  looks through your own collection for the pressing you actually own. The
  cover it shows is your sleeve, not a reissue's.
- **Play from the library** and it follows along, track by track.
- **Switch the turntable on** and the room becomes the turntable's. Switch
  it off and the room goes back to whatever else is playing. Nobody presses
  anything.

## Under the hood

A turntable, a few small devices, and a handful of services that meet at one
message bus. The [architecture](docs/architecture/) page has the whole
system in one picture, and every design choice is written down with what it
was chosen over.
